Summary of MULTI 2026 Workshop

The 2026 MULTI workshop has been a huge success and has sparked many interesting discussions. Both days of the workshop were well-attended with over 40 participants each day.

Summary of "Future of MULTI" Discussion

As is tradition for MULTI, the workshop closed with a discussion on the future of the workshop with community insiders and outsiders. Especially three aspects have been mentioned during the discussion that aim at increasing the visibility and adoption of MLM in academia and industry.

  1. Educational entry point for MLM. Multi-level modeling can be overwhelming at first for newcomers. A contributing factor to this is the broad heterogeneity of existing MLM languages that implement core modeling constructs differently. Over the years, the MULTI community has analyzed these differences and introduced a new terminology to label the different flavors of MLM. As a result, reading current papers and staying up-do-date with MULTI research is effortful and demanding for community outsiders. In the discussion, it has been pointed out that we must provide a centralized entry point for outsiders trying to familiarize themselves with MLM. We believe the people involved in the organization of next year's MULTI should prepare such an entry point by focusing on the core principles common to all MLM approaches. In the future, this may be expanded to include specifics about different MLM languages and approaches. Adding such specifics must be a community project, where each research group may contribute specifics and links to internal documentations.
  2. MULTI branding. We should also reflect upon our branding. One outsider suggested that the name "multi-level" may be unappealing. We discussed that it may be time to create a "MULTI Manifesto" that concisely communicates the key ideas and supposed benefits of MLM -- just like the Agile Manifesto did over 20 years ago. The contents of this manifesto can be discussed at the next MULTI. For this purpose, next year's organizers should collect suggestions from the MULTI community.
  3. Industrial cases of MLM. This year, the workshop benefited greatly from the contributions of industrial partners that have recognized the need for additional classification capabilities in their everyday business. Although industrial adoption of specific and advanced MLM principles remains sparse, it may be the time for MULTI to present and discuss actual industrial use cases and to compare industry solutions with academic solutions. One possible direction is to initiate a MULTI Industry Collaborative Comparison challenge, which extends the original MULTI Collaboration Challenge to industry partners.

Thanks to the PC, the paper authors, our keynote speaker, and all particpants for contributing to a successful MULTI 2026 workshop!
